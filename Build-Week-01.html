Build Week 01
& How We Got Here

Mon 23 - Sun 29 March

March 28, 2026
Scroll Down

It’s happening!! The build officially kicked off on Monday 23rd March. Woken up at 6:45am I was, to the sound of a truck rumbling past hehe It was Gloucester Valley Landscape Supplies with a little red digger on the back.

Lukas our builder arrived a few minutes later.

Went back to sleep for a bit. After my shower I had my usual morning walk around the house and tried to have a sticky-beak at what was happening up the hill. I could see the digger had created a big mound of grassy dirt that had been cut off the house pad. Couldn’t really get a good photo as I didn’t want them to see me watching hehe

Also exciting to see the progress of the pumpkins and my dahlia.

Around 11am, Pete had a look with his drone -

And another look around 2pm -

Not sure how many workers were here in total but they were here until around 3:45pm - a long day!! Pete got another aerial view then we headed up for a closer look.

Soooo exciting!! Hard to believe it was finally happening. The lead-up had been very stressful as around the 10th March the bank had requested the signed building contract, so we got the latest from Penny, all signed it and sent it through, thinking the bank was about to finalise the loan. Alarmingly, Karissa our mortgage broker informed us on Fri 20th that the bank could still take another 10-12 days - super stressful as the build was due to kick off on Monday and we had a 70K invoice due on the Wednesday!! Pete called Penny a number of times to try and discuss the situation and sent an email just before 5pm saying we could still start on Monday as that was already booked in, but that it might be best to wait on next steps until we had the loan in place - and that we’d be happy to cover any of their current expenses but asking if we could defer the larger payment until the bank got themselves sorted. Penny called a bit later and was very understanding and we agreed a way forward.

As it turned out, the bank ended up progressing things on Tuesday - setting up our new account and confirming full approval for the loan. While they are yet to make the first payment, it was a big relief to know the loan was fully approved. We could confidently pay our required contribution of $28K towards the first invoice, and felt better knowing Lukas & Penny would have more confidence in next steps as well.

We’ve been walking up to the new house site at least once a day, it’s all very exciting. Here are a few pictures from Tuesday - a foggy start to the morning, and still in love with my dahlia haha

The other big news from the week - we ordered the CFMoto UTV and it arrives tomorrow!! And we’ve signed a contract for the BYD Shark - TBC when that will be ready, but possibly in the next week. So much money flying out of our pockets, but so exciting!

The Tooting-Tosh-Mobile arrived today (Sunday)! Maybe I will call him Toto for short. He’s a CFMoto UFORCE 1000 UTV. I wanted to get something memorable with the $10K inheritance from Nana and Poppy, and I paid the rest with some of the money from the Personal Investment Plan set up by Aunty Kay and Uncle Trent on my 1st Birthday.

I was stressed all morning. Had an upset stomach and only got more stressed when the 11am scheduled arrival time passed. Around Midday I wondered if we should call someone, but their store wasn’t even open on Sundays so not much we could do. It was about 12:30 when Ben arrived -

He unloaded the vehicle then parked it next to the trailer and gave us a walk-through. He started with the winch, and the different buttons (lights, horn, blinkers). One thing that will take a bit of getting used to is unlike a car which you generally put in Park then put the hand brake on, with the UTV it’s best to put it in Neutral, apply the hand brake, then put it in Park.

We chatted for about 15mins then I had to sign some forms - didn’t really get a chance to read them properly and after later reviewing my own copy I’m pretty sure he got me to sign a page that was supposed to be signed by the assembly team, but what can you do!

After he left, we took Toto for a drive up the hill -

Of course we had to say hello to the cows - they were quite excited to see us - unfortunately we stopped recording but they followed us all the way to the next gate - I think they were quite upset we didn’t take them through to the next paddock with us!

I drove right over to the furthest-right corner of our block - somewhere I’d never actually been before.

We thought we might try and get across the creek up to the dam, and we attempted it - but there were some really hilly bits and I was really not enjoying it haha big wuss I am I guess. But I really didn’t want to roll it on the first day!! I’ll have to get more confident driving and work out the best approach to the creek. Currently the grass is quite long in parts, so you can’t see big holes until the last minute - was very close to a 2-3ft drop but luckily I spotted it. Headed roughly back the way we’d come, and over to the stand of trees. Swapped and let Pete drive for a bit and he drove us back to the house.

We’d originally planned to park Toto sideways in the shed (driving in the right side then turning left to nose it into the wall) - there was a lot of wire and other stuff in front of the gate on that side, which we’d hoped not to have to move. But once we got the vehicle in and saw where it was positioned, we realised the birds were probably going to shit all over it haha I had originally thought I could just put a cardboard box or something on the roof, but their nighttime spot was unfortunately positioned right above the windscreen haha

So while I was a slacker and had a bit of a rest, Pete tidied the front of the shed up so we could open the gate and back Toto in -

A bit later Pete convinced me to go for another drive. Mum had asked earlier if we could tow the bins down to the front when we needed to, but Pete had something else in mind -

I was a bit worried the trailer gate was going to break off haha We headed down towards the creek and as we went down the last slight dip the vehicle bunny-hopped a bit, how embarrassing. Hopefully neighbour Pete didn’t see haha

Had a bit of a walk around and was super excited to see a turtle!! It’s the first time we’ve spotted one on our property - not sure if it’s one we rescued a long time ago, or a new visitor.

Next Pete convinced me to give the winch a go, and pull out one of the old fence posts. It seemed to go OK.

Then drove around the side of the house via the paddock, back in the side gate and up the middle alleyway to the top of the hill - said hello to the cows again who were still quite interested in us. For some reason, they always seem to be less afraid of us when we’re in a vehicle than when we’re on foot.

Drove a bit towards the road side of the property, but not too far as it was hilly again. Stopped to pick up some rocks. We’d had them delivered ages ago and used to carry a couple back to the property whenever we walked down the hill, but we hadn’t been able to since the new fence was put in. It will be much easier now we have the buggy!

Dropped the rocks off, then drove around the front to hose some cow poo off (not sure we’ll do that every time as we don’t really have the water to spare!) Drove back to the shed and Pete directed me so I could back it in for the night.

Still feeling a bit apprehensive about driving it on some terrain, but I’ll get used to it I guess. Looking forward to being able to show Mum and Dad more of the block (but Mum will probably be even more nervous than me hehe)

I’ll throw to Pete now for his thoughts and he might talk about some of the hard work he put in marking out the house site last weekend. Actually Pete might take us back to where it all began - in London drawing up the plans!

This moment has been a long time coming. Years, in fact. Let me take you back to where it all started.

The Dream (London, 2023)

Amber and I always knew our move back to Australia meant going rural. My personal bar was 100 acres minimum - and if I’m honest, I spent more than a few evenings browsing properties closer to 1000 acres, which had Amber understandably asking what exactly I planned to do with all that land. She’s since come around. Once you’re out here, even the 500 metres to your nearest neighbour starts to feel a little close.

We were browsing realestate.com.au from the other side of the planet, trying to get a feel for what our budget could realistically get us. It didn’t take long to notice something interesting: a simple kit home on a decent block often came in at roughly the same price as many of the nicer established properties we were looking at. That realisation opened a door - if we were going to spend that kind of money anyway, why not build exactly what we wanted?

Which meant I needed to design it.

Building It in My Head First

I started teaching myself SolidWorks and Unreal Engine. I’d always been interested in Architecture growing up, and now I wanted to build our house - not pick from a catalogue.

After a few false starts and scrapped concepts, I settled on a single-storey design with a split skillion roof, incorporating a number of passive energy efficiency principles. The heart of the design was what I started calling the “Dining Hall” - a large, open kitchen and dining space that I wanted to feel genuinely grand without being wasteful. Getting the scale right was an obsession. Amber will tell you I developed a habit of quietly measuring restaurants and event venues whenever we were out - pacing out dimensions, doing mental maths, filing it all away for comparison.

Eventually, I got the model to a point where we could actually walk through it in VR. It was buggy, the performance wasn’t great, and it was finicky to get running - but standing inside a virtual version of a house that only existed in my head was something else. It made the scale real in a way that no floor plan on screen ever could.

Around that time I stumbled across a tour of a multi-million dollar home in LA on YouTube that had a remarkably similar layout to what I’d been designing. Completely independently arrived at. That was a good day.

Finding the One

We kept browsing properties as our move back to Australia got closer, and once we were home, we started doing actual inspections. We looked at a lot of land. Most of it was fine. Some of it was great. None of it stuck.

Then we got shown a place that didn’t quite fit our criteria on paper - priced somewhere awkward between our “with a house” and “without a house” budgets, with a seriously dilapidated farm shack on it that generously stretched the definition of “existing dwelling.” By any rational measure, it shouldn’t have been the one.

It was the one. Every property we looked at after that got compared to it. We stopped pretending otherwise pretty quickly.

From Napkin Sketches to Council Approval (July 2024 – April 2025)

Once we moved in, I reached out to a local builder - not with a formal brief, just to ask where you even start on a journey like this. He pointed us toward a design team out of Taree who were happy to take my rough SolidWorks model and turn it into something a council could actually assess. Andy came out on the 12th of July 2024 for an initial meeting and a walk of the block.

The back-and-forth process of turning years of self-taught design work into formal documentation was genuinely interesting. There was also some urgency: the local council was in the process of changing its rules around dwelling entitlements, and we needed our application submitted under the old rules, where existing dwellings carried an implicit entitlement for a new build. The new rules weren’t so clear, and we’d spoken to the head of the Council Department at a local infrastructure opening, and his advice was to get in quick. We got our Development Application in on the 7th of March 2025 and received approval on the 30th of April - just under a year from that first meeting with Andy.

The Part Nobody Warns You About

You’d be forgiven for thinking that Development Approval was the finish line. It isn’t even close.

What followed was a seemingly endless stream of additional applications, surveys, engineering reports and forms before a single piece of earth could be moved. Most of these were quick and easy. Mostly an excercise in watching money leave your bank account. The most gruelling of these was the Driveway Application - a process that started in early September 2025 and took almost five months to resolve. For context: the Development Application itself, which covered the entire house, took less than 3. Our crossover was finally approved on the 28th of January 2026.

In parallel, we’d been going back and forth with our builders finalising a detailed quote and working toward a contract. Then came the construction loan - and what I can only describe as the most stressful period of my life. We’d been pre-approved multiple times previously, so when our broker asked us to sign a substantial building contract, we did. Then we were told formal approval could still take weeks - with our first invoice due in days.

Our builders were understanding. We pushed the start date back a few weeks, had things escalated on the bank’s side, and eventually got the green light. We’re still yet to see a single dollar from the bank, but they’ve formally told the builders they can proceed - which has been enough to bring stress levels back down to merely “budgeting our finances for the next 30 years.”

Anticipation

We had our final chance to go up and inspect our chosen build site on the weekend. Amber and I spent a good portion of the day marking out the rough size and orientation of the build with marking paint, string, and some garden stakes.

High school geometry quickly came flooding back as I constructed circles and triangles and produced an outline that was only a little bit skew.

By the end of it, I’d outlined most of the exterior of the house, as well as a 5m work-zone around it for the builder. Walking around the layout like this made it feel small again, but I knew better than to trust my intuition - it would feel larger once it was built.

And Then the Digger Arrived

Monday morning. 6:45am. A truck rumbled past with a little red digger on the back.

Everything above - the late nights in London staring at floor plans, the VR walkthroughs, the council forms, the bank calls - it all led to that moment.
