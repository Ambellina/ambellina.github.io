Build Week 20

Mon 3 - Sun 9 Aug

August 9, 2026
Scroll Down

Mum and Dad had a quick trip home - only 3 hours! I was sad they’d gone and I had to get back to work :( I already had a heap of extra work due to the new role, and it was a loooong first shift today - 6hrs before my first proper break!

The workers were back today - the usuals, plus a couple of more interesting ones - Packer Bricklaying arrived at 8:09 and left at 8:40. Bucketts Building Supplies & Services arrived with some wood at 12:48 and left at 1pm.

We received an email from Penny - another decision to make -

Lukas called Pete and he went up for a chat. We’re going to change the external mudroom wall (where it sits against the ‘future build’ section) so that it’s brick rather than cladding. The roofer has also pushed things back AGAIN but luckily only a day this time - he’s coming on Wednesday and it’s expected we should have the roof on by the end of the week.

On my break I read a couple of chapters of my book - first in the sun, then in by the fire. What a life. Also wrote a little journal while I ate my lunch. The neighbours doggo was lurking again. Around 3:45 we headed up to the house site.

I’m used to Pete being annoying and getting in my photos, but I knew it must have reached a new level today when he said “you’re going to have to take that one again” haha I hadn’t noticed him at first but got a laugh when I zoomed in!

In other news, today I spotted this hole which has apparently been there for weeks haha and we have our eye on some of the post offcuts.

Inside, it looked like they’d made some additions to the frames.

On the walk back, I noticed the clouds were making interesting shapes, and our topsoil pile seems to be growing geraniums!

Back to work at 4:15. At 5 I had a handover call with Vix which was looooong and an overload of info, but she’s been doing some great stuff with AI that will really help the team. She also gave me her overview of the team, which was verrrrry enlightening. We chatted until about 6:45. Lee messaged me asking if I could join a call later, and to come with an “open mind” - we caught up at about 7:15 and he proposed I come over for 3 months to work on Manchester United to get things back on track, and that Stadion would put Pete and I up somewhere and it could be anywhere in the right timezone - meaning we could stay in South Africa or Spain or anywhere over that way. What an amazing opportunity, but with all the house stuff going on we’d have to think about it.

I ended up working nearly a 10hr day and I was buggered haha

Tues 4 Aug

Nothing much to report on Tuesday. Just another loooong work day and no site visitors.

Weds 5 Aug

Wednesday though it was all happening! Along with the regular arrivals we had Bennetts Metal Roofing at 6:56, a red Ute and trailer at 7:01, a grey Ute at 7:04 and a Bennetts truck at 7:08 who absolutely flew up the driveway haha There was a Pacific Swift (courier?) van at 7:59 who left about 10mins later. Someone (Lukas I think) had left in a truck at 7:22, returned at 8:09, left again at 9:03, returned in a different truck at 9:58, left again at 11:50 and returned at 1:23 - phew!

We also had a lovely Kooky visitor.

Most of the roofing guys seemed to leave at around 2:30, but Lukas’ team stuck around until closer to 4. Pete got his drone shot and we headed up at about 4:30.

We’ve got half a roof, how exciting!!

Some bits were a bit scatty, but we figured they’d get tidied. Hopefully it wouldn’t rain too much while some of the insulation was still exposed. From the outside it wasn’t much different, but from inside you could start to see how the lighting was changing.

They were also starting to prepare some of the window panels.

Lukas had also called Pete earlier in the day to ask about the architrave in the bathroom - due to the proximity to the bath and shower, he recommended we tile around the joint rather than use wood, which we thought would be fine.

We had a quick drive to see how things were looking from a distance.

At about 5:30 I took the bin down for collection and visited the horse with a carrot treat - she had some pats and ate out of my hand today and now we are best friends hehe

Thurs 6 Aug

Have I ever mentioned the shock I get sometimes when I see the feet Pete brings to bed? haha to be fair, I think they’re just permanently stained from his thongs rather than actually dirty.

Another big crew up at the site today, continuing on the roof. 7 vehicles headed up between 6:40 and 7:06. There was a delivery at 7:15 - everything was unloaded and they left again at 7:50. The plumber arrived at 8:47 and left at 9:25.

Pete and I headed in to town at about 1:30 to get some groceries. On the way home we drove up Beatties Island Road to see how the house was looking from that angle. I’d been wanting to drive to the end of that road now we had a Ute - we’d tried previously in the Scooby but had to reverse back up. I thought we’d be able to drive down to the train line, but unfortunately we got to a closed gate before we got too far, booooo! Actually the gate wasn’t actually latched and we thought we should be able to drive down under the train line, but there was a house just past the gate and we didn’t want to upset anyone, so we left. I had to reverse all the way back to Don’s place - Pete wanted me to use the cameras but I find it so weird trying to line up the yellow wheel lines, it all feels backwards and I would much rather just look out the back window like I used to! As we were heading back to Bundook Rd, Pete spotted a Falcon in a tree.

The roofers had headed off at around 2:15 but Lukas’ team stuck around for another hour. Pete took his drone shot and we headed up for a closer look. As well as the roof work, it was exciting to see the window panels going in!

With the roof on, it was nice to see we were still getting light in through the top windows. We also noted that the Hardiplank had arrived, and more work had been done getting some posts in.

As we were leaving, we noticed some leftover insulation had been disposed of in the skip, and we thought we might be able to use it down at the cottage.

Back at home I started to do some journal, but then got distracted when I remembered Penny’s email from Monday about the mortar choice. I asked Claude for some advice, based on our brick choice (Red Gum by PGH) -

How the main options tend to read on a rust-toned brick like this:

Natural/grey is the builder default and the safe option. It cools the brick slightly and mutes the red — you get a softer, more recessive wall. Nothing wrong with it, but it’s the one that does least.

Off-white gives the strongest contrast and makes the rust genuinely pop; off-white has become popular precisely because it emphasises the true colour of the brick. The trade-off is maintenance — white joints show dirt and weathering more, especially on south-facing or tree-shaded walls.

Charcoal is the one I’d look at hardest, because Red Gum carries that black fleck through the face. A charcoal joint picks the fleck up and ties the whole wall together, and it reads much more contemporary. Charcoal, black and dark greys have been trending for exactly this reason.

Buff/cream is the traditional pairing with red brick — warm, settled, slightly heritage. Softest contrast of the lot.

Two practical things. Mortar dries dramatically lighter than it looks wet, so never judge from a fresh joint. And build a real sample panel — ask your bricklayer for a metre or so in each shortlisted mortar, stood up on site, and look at it morning and afternoon. It costs almost nothing and it’s the only way to see the fleck interaction properly.

That was interesting and helpful feedback. We were leaning towards off-white at the moment. Now I needed to know more about joint styles.

Good choice — off-white will make that rust tone sing. Now joint profile matters even more, because the deeper the joint, the more shadow you throw across your off-white and the greyer it reads. PGH’s own selection guidance lists four: round ironed, flush, raked and struck.

We’d seen some examples of the joint styles when we’d visited PGH in Foster back in February with Mum and Dad, but none of us could seem to find a photo of the mortar sample wall. Luckily we located the brick book and saw that we’d circled ‘raked’. We had a photo of the Red Gum panel from PGH though the angle was a bit deceiving and some parts looked raked and some flush.

I replied to Penny -

I had 4 work calls on Thurs night, including a long scheduling meeting I have to run now Vix is leaving. But they all went fine.

Fri 7 Aug

5 vehicles arrived on Friday morning between 6:53 and 7:04.

I noticed on the cameras that the neighbours dog was hanging around again a bit after 1pm.

Around 2pm, Pete and I headed down to visit the horse and pick up a package - it was the new front bumper for Looby. We also saw a really cool bug on the letterbox - a weevil apparently.

When we got back up to the cottage, Pete got to work swapping Looby’s bumper over.

We’d also recently received some replacement front wheels but Pete couldn’t find them and asked me to help look. I looked in a few places but then the workers started going home and Lukas stopped for a chat. We met his offsider (Nash?) and the bloody neighbour’s doggo was hanging around again haha though he was mostly well behaved.

Lukas and his team (though not Nash, awkward!) would be going on a ski trip next week - I think Lukas and Penny were a bit worried we’d be upset due to the timing / delays, but it was booked ages ago and couldn’t be helped. Some sand would be arriving next week and the plumber would be around to do some fitout, but probably not much else would be happening. Lukas had heard a bit about our mortar email and said he’d mixed up some mortar samples up at the site for us to have a look at.

After we said our goodbyes (and enjoy the snow!) we walked up to the house site. We could see (first photo) where he’d marked out for the sand to be delivered. Things were generally looking a bit tidier now some flashing had gone up.

The shower wall had gone up - it was wider than we’d expected, though seems to be to spec. Hopefully the side passages wouldn’t be too narrow once the walls and tiles were finished.

We saw the top window frames had also arrived but not been installed yet. The cement colours were interesting though they were still too wet to really judge yet.

Around 8pm we made mini Hawaiian pizzas and watched the Roosters in a very strange match against the Doggies. It was all over the place and we were down 18-8 at one point, but pulled it together for an exciting win, woo!

Sat 8 Aug

Saturday I read Jackdaws in front of the fire for a bit, before taking a stroll in the garden and watering some of the plants. Then I settled in to catch up on some journalling.

Around 2:40 we headed in to town. First stop was the hardware store - I got a plastic plant saucer and Pete got a crayon, a spirit level and some conduit. He expertly secured the conduit and then we headed to Woolies.

I didn’t need anything and I thought Pete was just getting chocolate milk so I decided to wait in the car. He was taking so long I thought he must have been milking the cow, but he eventually returned with 15 items and $110 poorer haha so much for just getting milk! We’d debated having mini pizzas again for dinner as we’d quite enjoyed them the previous night, and Pete had picked out some extra ingredients so I could try a meatlovers combo that I’d mentioned - it was a great success!

Sun 9 Aug

Sunday was pretty laidback. I read some more of Jackdaws then spent most of the day catching up on the journal, while Pete played Minecraft.

We did have some random visitors - two women in a black Land Rover Defender drove up and turned around at the shed before leaving again. A bit strange. It’s quite a notable number plater - SXS - so I’m sure we’ll spot them again if they’re locals. We made sure to save the video just in case anything sus happens!

My anti-alarmist thinking is that they’re probably friends with the rich couple who recently moved in to David’s old place just up the road, and got lost trying to find it.

We had forgotten to check on the mortar yesterday, and now it was starting to rain so we had to duck up quick to see how it was looking.

I think we’re still leaning towards off-white, though it’s looking greyer than I expected. The white is looking less bright than I thought it would too. We meant to take our brick sample up to hold alongside the mortar examples, but we forgot - we’ll come back again when it’s not raining.
