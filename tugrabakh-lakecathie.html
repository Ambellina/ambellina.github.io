Day 1: Tugrabakh -> Lake Cathie

January 16, 2026
Scroll Down

Set off about 12:45.

Stopped at the Artisan Farmer to have a look. Felt like something bready (maybe focaccia) but they didn’t have anything like that today. Had a look at the other wares, thinking maybe I could find something for Mum’s Birthday, but no luck. Pete and I got a ‘Phoenix’ which is ‘Crispy fried chicken, sweet chilli chutney, creamy slaw & citrus aioli on a freshly made baguette’ - we got it to-go.

On the road again, about 50mins to our destination I think. Told Pete I was writing a journal and he jokingly said “yuck” haha

Near Bonny Hills we spotted some art on a big water tank in the distance, and made our way there to have a look.

Arrived at Little Palm Cabins about 3:30 - lots of cabins close together, which was not what I expected but fine. Our cabin is number 14. Inside there’s not a lot of room but it’s all we need. Couch has seen better days. Bathroom looks to have been newly renovated though.

Unpacked a bit and got to eat our lunch which was yum.

Looked through the leaflets of local attractions. Pete is trying to convince me to meet a monkey.

One of the other leaflets showed you all the koala sculptures you can see in the area. We intend to visit a few.

16:20 - we’re both quite tired but I think we’ll go for a drive.

Drove about 15mins back to North Haven, where we’d seen a sand bar / mud flat on the way past earlier. It would have been a bit deep for me to get out to in my clothes (up to belly height at least) but luckily Pete expertly piggy-backed me over. The water was quite warm. Nice views. Saw a sea eagle diving for fish. When we got back to the car, we saw him fly over with a fish in his talons. I sat on the rocks for a bit while Pete tried to get some photos. Heard a guy call out that there were some stingrays and we saw them flapping about.

Next visited North Haven Beach and Breakwall. Had a bit of a debate about breakwater vs breakwall1.

Saw some interesting signs.

Saw a house with heaps of animal statues. I got out to take a photo and Pete kept driving away when I tried to get back in the car, and I didn’t have any shoes on :( Mean thing #1 haha Then he drove around a roundabout twice. Can’t take him anywhere.

Next stop was the Bonny Hills Fire Brigade to see their beautiful mural. Pete pretended he had caught a little critter and wanted me to put my hands out so he could show me (like he knew my Dad used to do) but when I got close enough he threw it at me - it was just a leaf but it gave me a fright because I’d thought it was alive - that was mean thing #2 haha doesn’t he know it’s nearly my Birthday!

Had a look at Rainbow Beach. Drove around and contemplated some of the houses and their design choices - thinking about what we might have in our new build.

Headed up towards Port Macquarie and Tacking Point Lighthouse. The car started making funny noises. Was a bit better when we switched off the A/C. There was a nice view from the lighthouse and interesting markings in the sand.

About 7pm stopped at John Downes Park and inspected the car a bit. It seemed like something melted in one of the belts. Not really sure. Pete called Bill for advice and they decided that we shouldn’t drive the car anymore. Called some hire car companies but no luck. Calling NRMA now. There were tears haha

Booked NRMA for 60-90 mins. Booked a hire car to pick up from the airport tomorrow. 8:43 called NRMA to see where they were - apprently the callout had been cancelled, f***.

Got an Uber back to our cabin. At least we only had to wait 5 mins for him to come and only about 15mins to get home. Now sitting here sulking haha Didn’t get to go to Woolies before they closed. Nothing open in walking distance for dinner.

Joked with Pete that I’d be sad if his Birthday card for me didn’t have stickers on it. He showed me that he’d decorated it with pen instead - lots of stars and he’d drawn my favourite cow 240 :’) happy tears this time!

1 - Breakwaters and breakwalls are often used interchangeably to describe coastal structures that protect shorelines, harbours, and boats by breaking wave energy. While a “breakwater” is defined by its function of breaking waves, a “breakwall” often refers to the constructed, reinforced wall itself. Both are designed to reduce erosion, though breakwaters are typically offshore and breakwalls may be connected to the shore.
