Build Week 02

Mon 30 March - Sun 5 April

April 5, 2026
Scroll Down

Nobody came on Monday. We thought they might still do some work on the pad because it’s not quite flat yet, but I guess they’ll come another time.

In other news, the council is setting up a dog park down at Gloucester District Park! It will have separate areas for big and little doggos, and rumour-has-it, some agility equipment as well.

Tuesday saw a visit from Vicki and Bill on their way back from Mungo Brush. They arrived about 2pm and poor Bill was starving so we had lunch first and a chat. Later we took the buggy up to the house site for a look around. More relaxing and chatting back at the cottage before V&B headed home and I headed back to work.

The surveyors turned up on Wednesday. They were up at the site for a few hours and later when we went up they’d marked out a few more boundaries with posts.

It was another hectic day of phone calls - I think first was BYD asking about whether we were interested in financing the car purchase. It was a very good deal but we probably won’t go ahead with it as it might upset the bank if we get another loan right now. Next we had a call from a lady trying to arrange another property valuation - no idea why, when the loan was already approved and the cost had actually gone down. While we were up at the house site, we noticed missed calls from Karissa our mortgage broker, who was a bit panicked about something the bank had just spotted in our building contract - one section said the deposit couldn’t be included in the ‘staged costs’ but the staged costs added up to the total, so there was concern that there was a potential shortfall of 5%. We were fairly certain the deposit was included in the total, but tried to get in touch with Penny and Lukas to confirm. Karissa was pushing to find out asap because the bank was calling her back in about 40mins and she wanted to have it all in order so they could go ahead and make their first payment. Probably about 6 calls later it was all confirmed as OK and we’ll get a slight amendment to the contract so it’s clearer, but we don’t have any extra to pay luckily.

In the morning, the bank had sent LMD a “Builders Commencement Letter” advising that they’d approved our loan and that they were happy for construction to begin. Penny said Lukas was now lining everyone back up to get things moving on site again, including the excavator on Monday 14th April (which isn’t a real date, so I guess it will either be Monday 13th or Tuesday 14th!)

Not much to report from Thursday, except an update on pumpkin progress -

Our neighbour David also came to visit to see if we might like his lounges - he’s moving to Macquarie Park and wasn’t able to sell the lounges yet, but needs them gone. They might be useful for our new house while we save up for the ones we really want, but we don’t really have anywhere to store them at the moment, so we think we’ll pass.

There was a cute moment where David’s dog Focus and Willie were best friends.

We’ve been driving the buggy a little bit each day and slowly getting more used to it.

Today is Good Friday and we’re on holiday yay!! I trimmed a couple of things in the garden in the morning. My next Dahlias are coming along nicely -

Spent most of the day going through our finances and trying to plan out what accounts we need and how much money we’ll aim to put towards everything each month e.g. how much we need for the loan repayments, for day-to-day expenses, to set aside for entertainment / when people visit, to save for the bigger upcoming purchases like lounges, appliances etc. We think we’ve got a fair plan in place, but we’ll just have to see how it goes and adjust as needed.

Mid-arvo we took the buggy out, with the goal of finding a way across the creek. We investigated a few options and eventually found a way across, which was quite exciting. We headed up along the fence line, into the next paddock and over to the far corner to check out one of the dams.

The cows were waiting for us when we got back to the main fence juncture hehe

Worked on some more finance calculations, and then saw the cows out the front, including 240 near the fence, so I decided to offer her a few of our last bread slices. She was fine with me being nearby, but wouldn’t come any closer at first. I threw one bit of bread to her, then she got the second slice from my hand. Then she gave me a bit of a nuzzle and we are best friends again hehe

Nachos planned for dinner! Tomorrow we will probably go to the Barrington Hall markets.

On Saturday we did indeed visit the markets in Barrington. We entered the raffle but didn’t win as usual. Saw Adele who is always so smiley and friendly with everyone. Bought some Anzac biscuits. Contemplated buying some bread but it was a bit pricy (I think $10 was the cheapest loaf). At a previous market in Billabong Park just before Christmas, I’d bought a couple of coasters with Cows on them. At the time I was a bit miffed that they didn’t have any cows with happy expressions (I bought a sad cow and a surprised cow). Today I found a happy cow to add to the collection!

Next we headed to Gloucester Creatives who were holding a Trash & Treasure market. There was a fairly good turnout, but a lot more trash than treasure hehe The wares inside impressed as usual though. There were lots of things I would have liked to buy but nothing we really needed seeing we’re trying to save our pennies.

We went to Woolies and it wasn’t too busy. But I forgot the guacamole (we were planning to have Nachos for dinner again as we still had some leftover meat and cream) so had to head back in while Pete waited in the car - he was waiting a looooong time as I had to line up in the freezer aisle!!

Back home and checking out the garden - lots of capsicums on the way! The companion plants are also doing really well.

Happy Easter!! On Sunday we headed to the annual Duck races at Billabong Park. I wore my duck shirt especially hehe

It’s a great little community event. There wasn’t much water in the river this year, so they had a shorter course.

It absolutely poured just before the Easter egg hunt, which really cleared out the crowd. We headed back to our car (parked a way back near the oval) and then drove around to another spot, so we could walk back to view the last races from the bridge.

They had auctioned off some bigger ducks to race as well. There was a false start in that race hahaha

Back home, I spent more time preparing our 2-year Tugrabakh anniversary post.

Later in the garden we spotted these interesting growths, which Claude tells me are Bird’s Nest Fungi1. I was also happy to see the progress for my next 3 dahlias.

Later we went out in the buggy. We saw a dam we thought might be good to try yabbying again when Mum and Dad visit next weekend.

We went for a walk later in the afternoon and I found the best rock that looked just like a meatball. Pete and I had a few games of meatball bowling on the road, trying to get the meatball in a pothole, until one unlucky throw sent it off the edge and down the creek bank. Hopefully I’ll be able to find it again one day.

1 - From Claude - Those are bird’s nest fungi — a wonderful find! They’re a type of saprotrophic fungus (they decompose wood), which is why you’re seeing them thriving on that wood mulch.

Each little “nest” is a fruiting body called a peridium, and the dark lens-shaped discs inside are the peridioles — essentially spore packets. The design is beautifully clever: when a raindrop hits the cup at the right angle, it splashes the peridioles out, sometimes up to a metre away, and they stick to nearby plants or debris where they eventually release their spores.

From the photo, these look most like Cyathus species — possibly Cyathus olla or Cyathus striatus — though it’s hard to be 100% certain without a closer shot of the cup interior (striatus has distinctive vertical grooves on the inside walls). Both are common in mulched garden beds.

A few things worth knowing:

  • They’re completely harmless — not poisonous, not a plant pathogen. They won’t hurt your garden, your plants, pets, or kids. They’re actually a sign that your mulch is doing its job and breaking down nicely, feeding the soil ecosystem.
  • They tend to appear in flushes after damp weather and disappear once the mulch dries out or gets further decomposed. If you really don’t like the look, you can rake the mulch to disturb them, but honestly they’re one of the more charming things you can find in a garden.
